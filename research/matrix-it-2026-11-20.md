## Executive summary
- Israel's largest IT services and integration group: FY2025 revenue NIS 6.24B, about 11,570 employees, 80% of revenue from Israel [1][2].
- **The main trigger is the Magic Software merger, completed Feb–Mar 2026** [5]. The combined group has 17,000+ staff and NIS 8.4B pro-forma revenue [1], and two sales organizations that probably run separate CRMs *(inferred)*.
- **Matrix sells CRM itself.** It implements Microsoft Dynamics CRM and is listed as a Creatio partner [6][7]. So there are two ways to sell: (a) an internal CRM for the merged group, or (b) a reseller/implementation partnership. Expect the incumbent to be Dynamics *(inferred from the partnership)*.
- **Contact first:** CTO Ranit Zexer for the internal-CRM motion, or the head of Matrix's CRM/Dynamics practice for the partner motion (name not found).
- **Fit:** Medium–High. **Timing:** 6–12 months, while the post-merger integration is underway.

## Company brief
- **What they do:** IT consulting, systems integration, software development, cloud/infrastructure, outsourcing, AI projects, ERP (Priority, via Medatech), defense IT, and training (John Bryce) [8].
- **Industry / segment:** IT services for enterprise and government: finance, defense, public sector, industry.
- **HQ & footprint:** 3 Abba Eban Blvd, Herzliya. Israel 80.7% and US 16% of combined revenue [1][7]. US arm: Matrix USA, XTIVIA [8].
- **Size:**
  - Employees: about 11,570 for Matrix alone (Nov 2025) [2]; 17,000+ with Magic [1].
  - Revenue: NIS 6,239M in FY2025, up 11.8% [1]. H1 2026 revenue NIS 4,247M, up 4.3% (12.3% in constant currency) [4].
- **Ownership:** Public on the TASE. Formula Systems is the parent; Magic's former shareholders hold 31.1% after the merger [5].
- **Business model & go-to-market:** B2B and B2G project and outsourcing work. Matrix has grown through 80–100+ acquisitions since 2001 [8], so there are many business units and brands.
- **Recent developments:**
  - Aug 2026: Q2 net profit $45M, driven by cloud, AI and infrastructure. The CEO says fears that AI will hurt IT services firms are "disconnected from reality"; the shares were down about 40% year to date [3][4].
  - Jul 2026: agreed to buy 80% of Laor Energy for NIS 73M to expand its defense business; awaiting Competition Authority approval [10].
  - Feb–Mar 2026: Magic Software merger completed; Magic is now a Matrix subsidiary [5].

## Sales opportunities
1. **Unify the CRMs after the merger.** Evidence: the Magic merger closed in Feb 2026 [5]. → One CRM covering 17k staff, both companies' customer bases, and cross-selling between them. → Pitch: "Turn the merger into cross-sell revenue: one shared view of every customer across Matrix and Magic."
2. **A CRM that absorbs acquisitions fast.** Evidence: 80–100+ acquisitions [8], and Laor Energy is pending [10]. → A repeatable way to onboard each acquired company's customers and pipeline. → Pitch: "Get each new acquisition's pipeline into the group CRM within weeks."
3. **Cross-selling across divisions and key-account management.** Evidence: many divisions (defense, ERP, cloud, training, FinTech) selling to the same enterprise and government customers *(inferred)*. → Account planning across divisions, and pipeline visibility for the group. → Pitch: "See every division's work at your top 100 accounts in one place."
4. **AI-ready sales operations.** Evidence: the CEO is betting on AI as the growth engine; Matrix ran Israel's largest civilian AI project [3][4]. → Built-in AI for forecasting and deal intelligence, so Matrix uses internally what it sells to customers. → Pitch: "Do internally what you sell to your clients."
5. **Partner or reseller channel.** Evidence: Matrix already resells and implements Dynamics and Creatio [6][7]. → Add your CRM to its practice and reach its enterprise and government customers. → Pitch: margins and differentiation against Dynamics.

**Fit:** Medium–High. The size, merger and acquisition pace fit a CRM sale well; the main barrier is its Microsoft and Creatio partnerships.
**Timing:** 6–12 months. Integration decisions usually follow within the year after closing; the merger closed in Feb 2026.

## Suggested approach
- **Opening angle (to the CTO):** "With Magic now part of Matrix, you have two sales organizations and probably two CRMs serving overlapping enterprise customers. We help acquisitive IT services groups consolidate onto one platform and turn merger overlap into cross-sell pipeline. Worth 20 minutes to compare notes on your integration plan?"
- **Discovery questions:**
  1. Which CRM(s) do Matrix, Magic and the major divisions use today?
  2. Is there a CRM consolidation decision in your post-merger integration plan? Who owns it, and when?
  3. How do you onboard an acquired company's customers and pipeline today?
  4. How do you see and coordinate pipeline across divisions at shared accounts?
  5. Who owns sales operations at group level?
  6. How does your Dynamics partnership affect internal tool choices?
  7. Would your services arm consider implementing a new CRM platform for clients?
- **Likely objections:**
  - "We're a Microsoft partner and run Dynamics." → Position on time-to-value and handling multiple business units; offer a coexistence and integration path.
  - "We'll just build it ourselves." → Point to the total cost of ownership and the integration work already under way.
  - "The integration is already overloaded." → Offer a phased rollout, starting with cross-sell visibility.
- **Competitive notes:** Dynamics is the likely incumbent. Creatio is also in its partner portfolio. Salesforce is possible but unconfirmed.

## Risks & unknowns
- The internal CRM isn't confirmed (Dynamics is inferred); Magic's CRM is unknown.
- There's a conflict of interest: Matrix earns money implementing competing CRMs.
- Not found: group sales leader, CIO, and head of the CRM practice.
